# Integrity Case Study Workflow

Updated: September 2, 2026

## Purpose

Turn real Integrity jobs into trustworthy search content, sales proof, and linkable resources without inventing results or exposing customer information.

Case studies should explain the reasoning behind a diagnosis and solution. They are not generic “we fixed it” posts.

## Step 1: Flag a Strong Story

A job is a good candidate when it has at least three of these qualities:

- a common search symptom such as slipping, delayed engagement, no reverse, shudder, overheating, or limp mode;
- a recognizable transmission family such as 6L80/6L90, 10R80, 68RFE, 4L60E, 4L80E, or 700R4;
- clear diagnostic evidence;
- a meaningful choice between repair, rebuild, reman replacement, or declining the job;
- a towing, fleet, modified, or unusual duty-cycle consideration;
- useful photos or scan/teardown evidence;
- a completed outcome that can be checked later; or
- a lesson that could prevent a reader from ordering the wrong unit.

## Step 2: Capture the Job Record

Record the following while the details are still fresh:

### Vehicle and application

- year, make, model, trim, engine, and drive type;
- transmission family if confirmed;
- mileage range;
- relevant towing, work, tire, tuning, or power modifications;
- VIN for internal verification only—never publish the complete VIN.

### Customer complaint

- the customer's own description of the symptom;
- whether the issue was cold, hot, intermittent, speed/load dependent, or constant;
- warning lights, messages, noises, smells, leaks, or prior repairs;
- whether the vehicle arrived under its own power or by tow.

### Evidence

- diagnostic trouble codes and scan observations;
- fluid level/condition when relevant;
- pan, debris, filter, valve-body, clutch, converter, hard-part, cooler, or electrical findings;
- pressure or other measurements when performed;
- photos labeled by stage;
- evidence that ruled out alternative causes.

### Decision

- options explained to the customer;
- why repair, rebuild, reman replacement, or no work was recommended;
- supplier/package chosen if applicable;
- torque converter, cooler, fluid, programming, relearn, or installation requirements;
- warranty and core terms relevant to the decision.

### Outcome

- what was completed;
- delivery or completion timeline stated accurately, without turning one job into a blanket promise;
- initial verification/test result;
- customer-approved feedback;
- follow-up at a stated time or mileage interval when available.

## Step 3: Obtain Permission and Redact

Before publishing:

- obtain permission for vehicle photos, customer quotes, and identifying details;
- remove the full VIN, license plate, customer name, address, phone, email, repair-order number, and payment information;
- crop or blur reflections, documents, screens, key tags, and shop paperwork that reveal private data;
- use a general location such as “Springfield-area owner” unless the customer specifically approves more;
- never imply a warranty outcome beyond the written warranty terms.

## Step 4: Write the Story

Recommended page structure:

1. **Headline:** vehicle/application + real symptom + solution type.
2. **At a glance:** vehicle, mileage range, complaint, confirmed unit, and outcome.
3. **What the driver noticed:** the original complaint in plain language.
4. **What the evidence showed:** codes, inspection, teardown, and competing possibilities.
5. **Options considered:** repair, rebuild, reman, or other paths and their tradeoffs.
6. **Why this solution was selected:** application, use, budget, availability, warranty, and risk.
7. **What the work required:** converter, cooler, programming, installer, freight, core, or build details.
8. **Verified result:** immediate result and any later check, each with a real date or mileage interval.
9. **What another owner can learn:** symptom-specific lesson without claiming all vehicles fail the same way.
10. **Relevant next step:** link to the specific service, transmission page, symptom guide, or VIN quote.

## Step 5: Technical and Business Review

Before the page is indexable, confirm:

- every technical statement has been reviewed by the person who handled the job;
- the transmission designation and vehicle configuration are accurate;
- no private data remains in text, image metadata, or visible photos;
- timing, warranty, price, and outcome claims are job-specific and not generalized;
- the customer has approved any quotation attributed to them;
- the title and page intent do not duplicate an existing guide or service page;
- internal links point to the correct canonical URLs;
- the page has Article and Breadcrumb structured data;
- the hero and evidence photos are original to that case or clearly licensed.

## First Story Types to Confirm

These are potential story angles, not claims that completed cases already exist. Publish only if a matching real job and evidence can be verified.

- 2016-era Silverado 2500 / 6L90 with a documented complaint and reman-versus-rebuild decision.
- 700R4 for a higher-torque application where use and build choices changed the specification.
- 10L80 bench rebuild with clear teardown evidence and an installer-responsibility explanation.
- 68RFE towing application where duty cycle, tuning, cooling, or converter requirements mattered.

## Minimum Photo Set

Aim for five to eight useful originals:

- vehicle or application shot with private identifiers hidden;
- scan result or instrument view with private data removed;
- fluid/pan or external evidence;
- failed component or teardown detail;
- replacement/reman unit or rebuild stage;
- cooler/line/installation requirement when relevant;
- completed assembly or delivery;
- optional customer-approved vehicle photo after completion.

Photos should prove the story, not merely decorate it.

## Follow-Up Cadence

- Capture the intake and diagnostic evidence during the job.
- Draft within seven days of completion.
- Confirm immediate outcome before publication.
- Add a dated follow-up when the customer voluntarily provides one.
- Review the page after 90 days for queries, leads, links, and factual updates.
