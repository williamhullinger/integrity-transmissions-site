Meet The Owner

William Hullinger

Owner • Integrity Transmission & Drivetrain

I'm William Hullinger, the owner of Integrity Transmission & Drivetrain. My experience with transmissions didn't come from sitting behind a counter selling parts. It came from taking transmissions apart, finding out why they failed, rebuilding them, solving problems, sourcing hard-to-find components, and learning firsthand how much detail goes into getting one right.

Working inside transmissions also taught me something important: rebuilding isn't automatically the best answer simply because a transmission can be rebuilt.

Sometimes rebuilding the customer's original transmission is the right repair. Other times a professionally remanufactured transmission can provide a more comprehensive product, a stronger warranty, or simply better value for the money.

That experience shaped what Integrity is today. Instead of forcing every customer into one repair model, I want to be able to look at the vehicle, understand how it's used, compare the available options, and recommend the path that actually makes sense.

How Integrity Evolved

The more transmissions you work on, the more you realize there isn't one right answer.

Transmission rebuilding gives you a unique view of what actually happens inside these units. You see burned clutch packs, failed converters, worn valve bodies, damaged drums, broken hard parts, contamination, hydraulic problems, and the common weaknesses that show up repeatedly in certain transmission families.

You also see how quickly the economics of a rebuild can change when hard parts, electronics, hydraulic components, and other damage are added.

Over time, that made one thing increasingly clear: a good transmission business should not be limited to saying, “We rebuild transmissions.”

Customers should have access to rebuilding when it makes sense, remanufactured replacement when that provides a better product, upgraded builds when the vehicle demands more, and straightforward guidance when the right answer isn't immediately obvious.

Integrity Transmission & Drivetrain was shaped around that broader approach.

What hands-on experience teaches you

  • A failed component usually has a reason it failed
  • Not every reusable-looking part is actually within specification
  • Hydraulic problems can be as important as mechanical damage
  • Converters and contamination can destroy a fresh transmission
  • Known weak points should be addressed when appropriate
  • Vehicle use matters when planning a transmission build
  • Sometimes replacement is simply the smarter repair

Integrity Today

More than one way to solve a transmission problem.

Today, Integrity works with customers across a broad range of automatic transmission applications.

Depending on the vehicle and situation, that may mean sourcing and installing a professionally remanufactured transmission, rebuilding the original unit, performing a bench rebuild, planning a towing or performance build, or helping determine whether the problem is actually inside the transmission at all.

The business model is deliberately flexible because the right repair for a late-model daily driver may be very different from the right repair for a classic car, diesel work truck, towing vehicle, or modified street build.
