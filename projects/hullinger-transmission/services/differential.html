Services

Differential repair is precision setup work.

The differential allows the drive wheels to rotate at different speeds while carrying torque through the ring gear, pinion, bearings and carrier. Wear or incorrect setup can create whine, howl, growl, clunking, heat, leaks and eventually loss of drive.

A noise under acceleration can indicate a different contact pattern or bearing load than a noise on coast. Turning noise may involve carrier bearings, limited-slip components, wheel bearings or CV joints. The vehicle must be considered as a complete driveline before gears are condemned.

When gears or bearings are replaced, pinion depth, bearing preload, backlash and tooth contact pattern must be established correctly. Simply installing parts without measuring the setup can create immediate noise and short service life.

Identify the Axle Before Ordering

VIN information alone may not reveal every axle change or prior swap. Tags, casting numbers, gear ratio and differential type should be confirmed before parts are purchased.

What Matters

Evidence used in differential planning.

Noise, play, leakage and fluid condition help separate gear, bearing, axle and external driveline problems.

01

Whine & Howl

A pitch that changes between drive and coast can reflect gear contact, pinion bearings, backlash or wear from a previous setup.

  • Drive versus coast
  • Speed-related frequency
  • Recent gear or bearing work
02

Growl & Play

Carrier, pinion or wheel-bearing damage may create a growl that changes with load or turning. Excessive pinion or axle play adds important evidence.

  • Turning-load changes
  • Pinion flange movement
  • Axle and wheel-bearing checks
03

Leaks & Heat

Pinion, cover and axle-seal leaks can lower fluid or contaminate brake components. Heat and metal in the lubricant can indicate advanced damage.

  • Leak source
  • Correct fluid and additive
  • Vent and housing condition

Common Questions

Answers before you schedule.

These answers provide a starting point. The vehicle, evidence, written quote and warranty terms control the final recommendation.

What causes a differential to whine?

Possible causes include gear wear, incorrect backlash or pinion depth, bearing damage, low fluid and an existing poor contact pattern. The drive/coast pattern helps narrow it.

Can a wheel bearing sound like a differential?

Yes. Wheel bearings, tires, driveshafts and CV joints can produce speed-related noise that travels through the body and is mistaken for differential noise.

Can you change my gear ratio?

Selected ratio changes may be possible, but front and rear ratios must match on four-wheel-drive vehicles and calibration, carrier compatibility, tire size and intended use must be considered.

Do differential gears require a break-in?

New gear sets commonly require heat-cycle and fluid-service precautions. Follow the specific gear and installer instructions supplied with the completed job.
