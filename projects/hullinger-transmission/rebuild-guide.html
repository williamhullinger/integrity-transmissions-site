Transmission Rebuild Guide

What actually goes into rebuilding a transmission?

A professional transmission rebuild is more than installing a clutch kit and putting the unit back together. The transmission is disassembled, cleaned, inspected, measured, tested, corrected for known failure points, and rebuilt around the condition of the unit and the way the vehicle will actually be used.

The Rebuild Process

Inspection and measurement come before parts replacement.

Symptoms tell us what the vehicle is doing. Teardown and testing tell us what actually happened inside the transmission.

01

Complete Teardown

The transmission is disassembled so internal wear, contamination, broken components, friction damage, hydraulic problems, and hard-part condition can be evaluated.

02

Cleaning & Inspection

Components, passages, case areas, valve-body components, and reusable parts are cleaned before final inspection.

03

Measurement

Critical components are checked using appropriate measuring tools and procedures rather than relying only on visual inspection.

04

Testing

Depending on the transmission and component, hydraulic circuits, clutch circuits, valve-body areas, and apply components may be checked using vacuum testing, air testing, and other application-specific procedures.

Testing Matters

A transmission can look good and still have a problem.

Hydraulic leakage, worn valve bores, clutch-circuit problems, excessive clearances, and dimensional wear are not always obvious from visual inspection alone.

Depending on the unit, appropriate testing can include vacuum testing hydraulic areas, air checking clutch and apply circuits, checking end play and clutch clearances, and measuring reusable components with precision tools such as micrometers and related gauges.

Vacuum Testing

Helps identify excessive hydraulic leakage and wear in applicable valve-body circuits and bores.

Air Testing

Helps verify clutch packs, servos, pistons, seals, and apply circuits before final assembly where applicable.

Precision Measurement

Shafts, drums, bushings, clutch packs, clearances, end play, and other components are checked using the appropriate measurement method.

Assembly Verification

Clearances, component placement, fastener torque, check-ball locations, hydraulic components, and transmission-specific assembly details are verified before completion.

What's Included In A Rebuild?

Soft parts and hard parts are not the same thing.

Understanding that difference explains why two transmissions can receive the same basic rebuild package but end up with very different final repair costs.

Standard Rebuild Components

Soft Parts

Soft parts are the normal wear and sealing components that are typically replaced during a professional transmission rebuild.

  • Friction clutches
  • Transmission seals and sealing rings
  • Gaskets and O-rings
  • Filter
  • Application-specific rebuild components
  • Steels where required or included in the selected build
Important: A standard rebuild package replaces normal rebuild components. It does not automatically mean every component inside the transmission is replaced.

Major Mechanical Components

Hard Parts

Hard parts are durable mechanical components that are inspected and reused when they remain within acceptable condition.

  • Drums and hubs
  • Planetary gearsets
  • Shafts
  • Pumps and pump components
  • Cases
  • Sprags and one-way clutch components
  • Valve bodies and hydraulic components
  • Other transmission-specific mechanical parts
Important: Damaged or non-reusable hard parts are generally additional unless specifically included in the quoted build package.

Why The Difference Matters

Rebuild vs. Reman

A conventional rebuild and a professionally remanufactured transmission are two different repair models.

  • A rebuild starts with your existing transmission
  • Standard rebuild components are replaced
  • Reusable hard parts may remain in service
  • Damaged hard parts can increase the final rebuild cost
  • Remanufacturing may include broader standardized component replacement and qualification
  • Remanufactured units may also include broader testing and warranty programs
The right choice depends on: Transmission type, vehicle age, damage, replacement availability, cost, warranty, and long-term plans for the vehicle.

Common Failure-Point Corrections

We don't just replace what burned up. We look for why it failed.

Every transmission family has its own weaknesses. Where appropriate, known failure points are addressed as part of the rebuild so the transmission is not simply returned to the exact condition that contributed to the original failure.

Hydraulic Weak Points

Depending on the unit, worn valve-body areas, pressure-control issues, leakage, separator-plate problems, accumulators, servos, and other hydraulic components may need correction.

Mechanical Weak Points

Some transmission families have known weaknesses involving shells, drums, shafts, sprags, clutch housings, planetary assemblies, or other mechanical components.

Clutch Capacity

Certain applications can benefit from improved friction materials, clutch capacity, or transmission-specific changes that help increase holding capability under load.

Converter & Heat

Converter problems and excessive heat can destroy an otherwise good transmission. Converter selection, cooling, contamination, and fluid condition are considered as part of the total repair.

Custom Transmission Builds

From stock replacement to heavy-duty and performance builds.

Not every transmission needs every upgrade. We can tailor the build around the transmission platform, vehicle, power level, load, driving style, and intended use.

Factory-Type Use

Standard / Stock

Designed for normal daily driving at or near factory power levels.

  • Standard rebuild components
  • Inspection and testing
  • Common failure-point corrections where appropriate
  • Factory-style drivability
  • Application-matched converter direction
Best for: Stock daily drivers and vehicles that do not need additional torque capacity or heavy-use upgrades.

Durability Focus

Longevity / Reliability

Built for customers who want to address additional weak points while the transmission is already apart.

  • Everything appropriate to the standard rebuild
  • Additional known weak-point upgrades where available
  • Improved friction or holding capacity where appropriate
  • Hydraulic improvements where beneficial
  • Upgraded mechanical components when justified
  • Converter selected around long-term use
Best for: Long-term ownership, work vehicles, higher-mileage vehicles, and customers who want added durability without building a race transmission.

Load & Heat Management

Towing / Heavy Duty

Built around sustained load, additional heat, vehicle weight, and the torque demands created by towing and work use.

  • Increased holding capacity where available
  • Transmission-specific hydraulic improvements
  • Known mechanical weak-point upgrades
  • Heavy-duty converter considerations
  • Cooling and fluid recommendations
  • Vehicle weight and trailer load considered
Examples: 68RFE work trucks, Ford 4R100 or 6R80 towing applications, GM 4L80E/6L90 heavy-use vehicles, and other transmission-specific setups.

Modified Street Vehicles

Street Performance

Designed for vehicles making more than factory power while maintaining useful street drivability.

  • Additional clutch capacity where available
  • Hydraulic and valve-body modifications where appropriate
  • Converter matched to engine and vehicle combination
  • Available upgraded hard parts
  • Shift calibration based on intended use
  • Power level and vehicle weight considered
Examples: Modified 4L60E or 4L80E street cars, Ford 4R70W or 6R80 performance vehicles, and Dodge/Ram builds requiring more capacity than a stock transmission.

Higher Power Applications

High Performance

Higher-performance builds use a more aggressive component strategy based on the practical capability of the transmission platform.

  • Maximum practical clutch capacity
  • Available upgraded drums, shafts, sprags, or planets
  • Transmission-specific billet components where justified
  • Performance converter selection
  • Hydraulic and calibration changes
  • Power, torque, weight, gearing, and tire considered together
Best for: Higher-horsepower street builds and performance vehicles where the transmission must hold significantly more than factory torque.

Platform Capability

Maximum-Effort Build

For customers who want to push a transmission family toward the practical limits of what the platform can reasonably support.

  • Transmission-specific maximum upgrade strategy
  • Available billet and heavy-duty hard parts
  • Maximum appropriate clutch capacity
  • Performance hydraulic calibration
  • Application-specific converter
  • Supporting vehicle setup reviewed carefully
Important: Every transmission platform has a different practical capability. We will not represent every transmission as capable of supporting the same horsepower or torque.

What Do Transmission Upgrades Actually Do?

Parts lists matter less than what the upgrades accomplish.

Available upgrades vary by transmission. The goal is not to install expensive parts simply because they exist. The goal is to improve the areas that matter for the customer's application.

More Holding Capacity

Additional clutch capacity, improved friction materials, and transmission-specific changes can help increase torque capacity and reduce clutch slip under load.

Better Hydraulic Control

Valve-body and hydraulic improvements can help address leakage, pressure delivery, clutch apply characteristics, and known transmission-specific hydraulic weaknesses.

Stronger Mechanical Components

Upgraded shafts, drums, shells, sprags, planets, hubs, and other hard parts can improve resistance to known mechanical failures where stronger components are available.

Better Heat Management

Proper converter selection, cooling, lubrication improvements, fluid choice, and reducing unnecessary clutch slip can help control heat in towing, work, and performance applications.

Transmission Rebuild Questions

Common questions before choosing a build.

Does a rebuild replace everything inside the transmission?

No. A standard rebuild replaces normal rebuild components and wear items. Reusable hard parts are inspected and retained when acceptable. Damaged hard parts are generally additional unless specifically included in the build package.

What are soft parts?

Soft parts are normal wear and sealing components such as friction clutches, seals, sealing rings, gaskets, O-rings, filters, and other application-specific rebuild components.

What are hard parts?

Hard parts are major mechanical components such as drums, planetary assemblies, shafts, pumps, cases, hubs, sprags, valve bodies, and other durable internal transmission components.

Do you correct known transmission failure points?

Yes. Known failure areas are considered during every rebuild. The exact corrections depend on the transmission family, internal condition, and intended use.

Can you build a transmission for towing?

Yes. Towing-oriented builds can be tailored around vehicle weight, trailer load, converter requirements, heat management, hydraulic control, clutch capacity, and available transmission-specific upgrades.

Can you build for more horsepower?

Yes. Available performance upgrades depend on the transmission. Tell us the estimated horsepower and torque, vehicle weight, intended use, and planned modifications so the build can be matched to the application.

Does a stronger transmission have to shift hard?

No. Shift feel can often be tailored around the intended use. A reliability-focused street transmission does not necessarily need harsh shifts simply because stronger components or hydraulic improvements are used.

Can you quote a rebuild before teardown?

We can provide a starting quote based on the expected rebuild scope. Internal damage, hard parts, contamination, prior repair attempts, and other conditions discovered during teardown can change the final repair cost.
